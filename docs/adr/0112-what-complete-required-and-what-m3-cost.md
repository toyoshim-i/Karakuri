# What "complete" required, and what M3 cost

## Context

Asked whether M3 was *completely* finished, having just reported it closed.

## Decision

Yes — **and four things had to be fixed to make that sentence true.**

**"Everything is struck through" and "the section is true" are different claims.** The Adds list was
fully struck while the section's prose still carried four sentences written mid-milestone: one
pointing at remaining work that was struck forty lines below it; one that ended at *addressing the
language does not have* **without saying where it goes**; a demand — *capacity must be Set-level* —
that is **now false**, since each source runs at its own declaration, and which had to be recorded as
*met and then overtaken* (what it really demanded was that capacity not be baked into a compiled
artifact, and that still holds); and a note asking that layout compilation be **absorbed rather than
placed beside** the contract, which had in fact been absorbed.

Four unfinished things **inside** struck items were struck **with their destinations written**, and
because three are one job, M4 schedules it once.

## The accounting M1 asked for

M1 required this as *the only evidence for how to read later estimates*: **64 commits, 21000 lines,
9 test files, 9 examples, 844 tests. Implementation was again not the expensive half.**

**A `.kir` that passed every check took the process down six times.** The section already listed five
and named the shape they share — a feature widens what the checker accepts, and somewhere the widened
thing can reach there is nothing to produce. **The sixth arrived after that paragraph was written**,
in the final day's audit. Being able to say the shape out loud did not prevent it; the paragraph did
its job rather than contradicting itself.

Three reviews found about fifteen, the closing audit five more, and **the recurring defect now has a
name: one fact derived in two places.**

The tests also learned three things about their own material: a fixture smaller than a workgroup
cannot see a dispatch-count error; a freshly allocated buffer is zero, so *never written* and
*written correctly* draw the same picture; and a measurement has to be of a kind that can see the
defect it is aimed at.

## One thing deliberately not done, recorded as a decision rather than a plan

**The element slot was still 16-byte padded**, and M1 had written that the bill would come due at
M2's deck. **Two milestones later it had not**, because nothing forced it — four resident slots at
262144 elements do not exhaust memory here. Rewritten as **parked, not forgotten**, and what
schedules it is **a Set that does not fit, not a milestone boundary.**
